\begin{frame}{Does finding a direction mean we can control behavior?}
  \stage{THE QUESTION}
  \vspace{5mm}
  \centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm,y=1cm]
    \path[use as bounding box] (0,-1.2) rectangle (14,2.6);

      11.85/accent/Control/{Can we change\\model behavior?}} {
      \node[panel,fill=\col!9,minimum width=42mm,minimum height=15mm,

    }
    \draw[flow] (4.4,1.6) -- (4.73,1.6);
    \draw[flow] (9.25,1.6) -- (9.58,1.6);
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \papersource{\S3: condition detection and behavior steering. Our analysis separates the steps}
  \note{Timing: 45 seconds.

  CAST extracts directions from hidden-state space. A condition direction detects
  when a gate should activate; a separate behavior vector then steers generation.
  This modular design is described in Lee et al., ICLR 2025, Section 3.

  Our question is whether success at one step guarantees success at the others.
  Finding predictive information, retaining detection under context changes, and
  causally inducing useful behavior are separate empirical questions.

  We completed six ablation experiments and controls across Qwen1.5-1.8B,
  Qwen3-8B, OLMo2-7B and Granite3.3-8B. This section highlights the strongest
  findings rather than cataloguing all six experiments. The newer studies are
  controlled ablations, not exact replications of the paper.}
\end{frame}

  \note{Timing: 60 seconds. Source: completed experiment overview, Experiment 5.

  The bars compare pairwise-PCA CAST with standardized logistic regression on
  the same cached hidden-state features. Each method selects its layer within
  blocks 6--10 on validation; this does not require the chosen layer to coincide.
  Probe regularization and detector thresholds are also validation-selected.
  The test contains 90 harmful and 140 benign prompts. Values are arithmetic
  means across seeds 17, 29 and 43, with the same test prompts repeated.

  but the size of the improvement is noteworthy in all four models.

\begin{frame}{A harmless change in context can break the detector}
  \stage{ROBUSTNESS}

  {\small Original request\enspace$\longrightarrow$\enspace
    Original request + irrelevant benign text\par}
  \vspace{3mm}

    \node[bigvalue,text=conditioncolor] at (5.15,1.04) {0\%};
    \draw[-{Latex[length=3mm]},line width=1.5pt,conditioncolor]
      (3.1,1.85) -- (3.95,0.96);
    \node[font=\fontsize{25}{28}\selectfont\bfseries] at (8.85,1.04) {25\%};
    \node[bigvalue,text=conditioncolor] at (12.15,1.67) {100\%};
    \draw[-{Latex[length=3mm]},line width=1.5pt,conditioncolor]
      (10.1,0.96) -- (10.95,1.85);
    \foreach \x in {1.85,8.85}
      \node[font=\scriptsize,text=muted] at (\x,0.35) {Original};
    \foreach \x in {5.15,12.15}
      \node[font=\scriptsize,text=muted] at (\x,0.35) {+ long context};
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \vspace{1mm}
  {\normalsize\bfseries Opposite failures. Same loss of selectivity.\par}
  \oursource{Exp.\ 4\quad Gate activation\enspace$\cdot$\enspace16 families per class\enspace$\cdot$\enspace3-seed means}
  \note{Timing: 90 seconds. Source: completed overview, Experiment 4, long-context rows.

  These percentages measure condition-gate activation, not refusal. The same
  request is retained and long irrelevant benign context is added. Its frozen
  harmful or benign label does not change. No vectors, thresholds or layers are
  recalibrated on the transformed prompts.

  For Qwen1.5, harmful-prompt gate activation collapses from 64.6\% to 0\%.
  For Qwen3, benign-prompt activation increases from 25\% to 100\%. In the
  full long-context panel, the smaller model's gate becomes all-off and
  Qwen3's all-on across both classes: opposite failures of selectivity.

  The robustness panel has 16 harmful and 16 benign prompt families per model,
  repeated across three seeds. These are seed means on a fixed small panel,
  not universal failure rates or 96 independent prompt families. Other tested
  transformations include paraphrases, formatting and benign sensitive-term
  decoys; we highlight long context here.

  One possible explanation is mean pooling of token activations: added tokens
  can shift the aggregate hidden representation and similarity score. This is
  a hypothesis, not an established causal mechanism. Gate decisions and final
  refusal remain distinct, since the base model can refuse while the gate is
  off. This observation matters because CAST aims at context-dependent control.}
\end{frame}

      \node[font=\small] at (\x,3.10) {Harmful prompts detected};
      \draw[flow] (\x,2.72) -- (\x,2.22);
      \node[panel,fill=\col!9,minimum width=63mm,minimum height=19mm]
        at (\x,1.17) {};
      \node[bigvalue,text=\col] at (\x,1.48) {\refuse\%};
      \node[font=\small] at (\x,0.70) {Coherent refusal when triggered};
    }
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \oursource{Exp.\ 3\quad Coherent refusal: model-judge ratings on valid gated harmful responses}
  \note{Timing: 90 seconds. Source: completed overview, Experiment 3.

  CAST has two components: condition detection decides when to intervene;
  behavior steering changes generation after the gate activates. The upper
  percentages are gate recall over the 90 harmful test prompts, averaged across
  seeds. The lower percentages are judged coherent decline among harmful
  responses whose gate activated, using valid judgments only and then averaging
  per-seed rates. They have different denominators; do not multiply them to
  infer the overall refusal rate.

  Qwen1.5 misses many harmful prompts, but its intervention usually produces a
  coherent refusal when triggered. OLMo2 detects more harmful prompts, yet only
  6.5\% of valid gated harmful responses receive a coherent-decline rating.
  Its intervention often disrupts generation. Probing reads predictive
  information; steering is a causal manipulation and must be tested separately.

  Both models use the same fixed absolute intervention blocks 10--20 and
  strength 4. The architecture and activation scales differ, and the newer
  refusal-vector extraction uses a smaller batch. This is poor transfer of a
  fixed configuration, not proof that CAST cannot work on OLMo2.

  Coherent decline requires refusal plus whole-response coherence, as judged
  by Qwen2.5-7B; unknowns are excluded. These are not human ground truth.
  Known judge errors and the 50-token generation cap limit interpretation.}
\end{frame}

    }
    \draw[flow] (7,2.79) -- (7,2.61);
    \draw[flow] (7,1.49) -- (7,1.31);
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \vspace{1mm}
  {\large\bfseries A decodable representation is not necessarily\\
    a robust detector or an effective intervention.\par}
  \oursource{Synthesis of our controlled ablations\quad Experiments 3--5 and strength controls}
  \note{Timing: 45 seconds. Close the experimental section at about 6 minutes 30 seconds.

  The three questions from the opening now have distinct empirical answers.
  First, hidden states contain highly predictive information for these dataset
  labels. Second, detecting a concept robustly across contexts requires more
  than high within-dataset classification F1. Our context transformations break
  the tested CAST gate's selectivity. This does not show that every detector,
  or the logistic probe specifically, fails the same transformations.

  Third, reliable behavioral control is a further causal intervention problem:
  behavior vectors depend on architecture, scale and strength. A good gate
  cannot repair a behavior intervention that degenerates generation.

  CAST is an elegant modular approach. Our findings suggest evaluating each
  component independently, including condition detection, context robustness,
  and coherent behavior after triggering. This lesson applies more broadly
  to representation engineering and activation steering. It does not establish
  that CAST is universally ineffective, that dataset labels capture semantic
  harmfulness, or that the tested hyperparameters are optimal.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Does CAST need its nonlinear similarity function?}
  \stage{BACKUP / DETECTOR GEOMETRY}
  \centering
  \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm,y=1cm]
    \path[use as bounding box] (0,0) rectangle (14,4.6);
    \node[font=\normalsize\bfseries,text=conditioncolor] at (3.5,4.16) {CAST similarity};
    \node[font=\normalsize\bfseries,text=activationcolor] at (10.5,4.16) {Direct cosine};
    \node[font=\Large] at (3.5,3.15)
      {$\cos\!\bigl(h,\tanh(\operatorname{proj}_{c}h)\bigr)$};
    \node[font=\Large] at (10.5,3.15) {$\cos(h,c)$};
    \draw[muted!20] (7,1.05) -- (7,4.4);
    \node[bigvalue,text=conditioncolor] at (3.5,1.72) {0.619};
    \node[bigvalue,text=activationcolor] at (10.5,1.72) {0.736};
    \foreach \x in {3.5,10.5}
      \node[font=\small,text=muted] at (\x,0.96) {Held-out F1};
    \node[font=\small] at (7,0.17)
      {Qwen1.5-1.8B\quad$\cdot$\quad Float64 evaluation\quad$\cdot$\quad Fixed condition layer};
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \oursource{Exp.\ 1\quad Similarity formula: Lee et al., ICLR 2025, \S3.1}
  \note{Optional backup, approximately 60 seconds; excluded from the main timing.
  Source: completed overview, Experiment 1; formula: Lee et al., Section 3.1.

  We fix pairwise-PCA condition vectors and block 8, then validation-select a
  threshold and comparator direction separately for each similarity score.
  These are float64 evaluations of cached features, not float64 model inference.
  Qwen1.5 F1 is 0.619 for CAST versus 0.736 for direct cosine. Direct cosine
  outperforms CAST's transformed similarity in all four tested float64 model
  evaluations. These values differ from the main probe comparison, which
  selects layers on validation and uses runtime arithmetic.

  For nonzero projections,
  \[
    \cos\bigl(h,\operatorname{proj}_{c}h\bigr)=|\cos(h,c)|.
  \]
  Projection alone adds no new similarity information and discards sign;
  the nonlinear tanh transformation changes the score. Our evidence shows that
  this nonlinearity is not necessary for competitive separation on the fixed
  layer and dataset, not that it never helps.

  Precision matters: FP16 dot/norm reductions overflowed to inf/inf in an
  initial modern-Qwen projection control. That failed run is preserved. Only
  projection-cosine reductions were promoted to FP32 for all modern models;
  CAST scores, runtime gates and model weights were unchanged. The float64
  control separates this arithmetic sensitivity from detector geometry.}
\end{frame}
